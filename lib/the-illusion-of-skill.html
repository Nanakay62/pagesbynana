The Illusion of Skill

How Free Mobile Games Secretly Rig Your Screen for Ad Money

By Joe

When you sit down to play a free mobile puzzle game like Block Blast, the rules seem simple. You get random blocks, you fit them onto a grid, and how far you go depends entirely on your focus and strategy.

Behind the colorful graphics, however, something very different is happening. According to a reverse-engineering of Block Blast, a game that generates over $100 million a year in ad revenue and out-earns giants like Candy Crush, it achieves this massive payout not by testing your puzzle-solving skills, but by secretly rigging your wins and losses around how much money advertisers are willing to pay for your attention.

STEP 1

Putting a Price Tag on the Player

From the moment you open the app, the game is quietly sizing you up. Before deciding which puzzle pieces to hand you, a built-in program running directly on your phone collects specific clues about you:

  • Your Device and Location: The game checks what kind of phone you own and what country you live in, since advertisers pay much more to reach people in wealthier regions or those using expensive phones.
  • Your Past Behavior: It tracks how much money your past ad views have earned the company and whether you are in the habit of watching ads to keep playing.
  • Your Phone Settings: It even checks tiny behavioral habits, like whether you have turned off your phone’s vibration settings.

All of these details are combined to calculate a single prediction: how much cash you are worth to the game right now. Once the app knows your price tag, it changes the difficulty of the game to match.

STEP 2

The “Boost Then Kill” Trap

If the game sees that you are a valuable player, it does not want you to lose early and close the app. Instead, it activates a hidden rule called “Boost Then Kill.”

During the “Boost” phase, the game secretly feeds you helpful, easy-to-place blocks. You clear line after line, convincing yourself that you are playing the best game of your life. In reality, the game is simply keeping you entertained until you reach the average amount of time you normally play.

While you are enjoying your winning streak, the app is waiting in the background for a high-paying advertisement to load. If the game confirms that a premium ad is ready and knows from your history that you are willing to watch videos to save your progress, it moves to the next phase: “High-Value Ad Harvest.” The app deliberately helps you push right past your personal high score so that you feel emotionally invested in the round.

STEP 3

The Death Algorithm and the Paid Rescue

Right when you are having the most fun and are terrified of losing your record-breaking run, the game turns on you. It triggers what the code refers to as the “Death Algorithm.”

Instead of giving you random or helpful blocks, the game deals you an impossible combination of pieces that cannot fit on your board, guaranteeing that you lose on that exact turn.

The moment the “Game Over” screen appears, the trap snaps shut. The very first thing you see is an offer to revive your game and save your record run if you watch a short video. That video just happens to be the expensive advertisement the game was holding onto moments earlier. Because the game helped you get so close to a milestone before forcing you to fail, you are far more likely to cave in and watch the commercial.

STEP 4

The Reward That Keeps You Hooked

If you take the bait and watch the ad to revive yourself, the manipulation continues. To make sure you feel like watching the commercial was worth your time, the game secretly inflates your high score by an extra 10 percent. You walk away with a rush of accomplishment, making it much more likely that you will come back tomorrow and fall for the exact same cycle.

To perfect this system, the developers constantly run background experiments on players, testing the exact minute to force a loss to see which timing convinces the most people to watch the revive ad.

The Takeaway for Everyday Players

What looks like a harmless, relaxing brain teaser on the surface is actually an automated digital carnival game. Just like a booth operator who lets you win the first two tosses so you will pay cash for one last try at the giant prize, modern free-to-play games manufacture your highs and lows on purpose.

The next time you lose a puzzle game right before beating your record, remember that you probably did not make a mistake. A high-paying commercial simply finished loading in the background.
